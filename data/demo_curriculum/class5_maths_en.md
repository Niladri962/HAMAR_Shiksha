## Chapter 1: Place Value

Every digit in a number has a place. In the number 4,352 the digit 4 is in the thousands place, so its value is 4,000. The digit 3 is in the hundreds place and is worth 300. The digit 5 is in the tens place and is worth 50, and the digit 2 is in the ones place and is worth 2.

Writing a number as the sum of the values of its digits is called the expanded form. The expanded form of 4,352 is 4000 + 300 + 50 + 2.

The place value of a digit depends on its position, but the face value of a digit is the digit itself. In 5,742 the face value of 7 is 7, while its place value is 700.

## Chapter 1: Place Value

To compare two numbers with the same number of digits, start from the left. Compare the thousands digits first; if they are equal, compare the hundreds digits, then the tens, then the ones. For example 9,990 is greater than 9,909 because the tens digit 9 is greater than 0.

In India we use commas after the hundreds place and then after every two digits: 1,00,000 is one lakh. In the international system the same number is written 100,000 and read as one hundred thousand.

Practice: Write the expanded form of 3,405. Answer: 3000 + 400 + 5. There is no tens, so we write 0 in the tens place.

## Chapter 2: Fractions

A fraction shows a part of a whole. In the fraction 3/4, the number below the line is the denominator. It tells us into how many equal parts the whole is divided. The number above the line is the numerator. It tells us how many of those equal parts we take.

Example: a roti is cut into 4 equal pieces. If Meena eats 3 pieces, she has eaten 3/4 of the roti. The parts must be equal; if the pieces are of different sizes, we cannot use a fraction to describe them.

A fraction whose numerator is smaller than its denominator, such as 2/5, is called a proper fraction. It is less than one whole.

## Chapter 2: Fractions

Comparing fractions with the same denominator: when the denominators are equal, the fraction with the bigger numerator is bigger. So 3/4 is greater than 1/4, because 3 parts out of 4 is more than 1 part out of 4.

Comparing fractions with the same numerator: when the numerators are equal, the fraction with the smaller denominator is bigger, because the whole is cut into fewer, larger pieces. So 1/3 is greater than 1/5.

A common mistake is to think that 1/5 is bigger than 1/3 because 5 is bigger than 3. Remember: more pieces means each piece is smaller.

## Chapter 2: Fractions

Equivalent fractions name the same amount. If we multiply or divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number, we get an equivalent fraction. For example 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6, and 6/8 = 3/4.

Adding fractions with the same denominator: add the numerators and keep the denominator the same. For example 1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5, and 1/2 + 1/2 = 2/2 = 1 whole.

Word problem: A mango is cut into 8 equal pieces and Ravi eats 3 of them. The fraction eaten is 3/8 and the fraction left is 5/8, because 8/8 - 3/8 = 5/8.

## Chapter 3: Measurement

Length is measured in metres (m), centimetres (cm) and kilometres (km). 1 metre = 100 centimetres and 1 kilometre = 1000 metres. To change metres into centimetres, multiply by 100: 2 metres = 200 centimetres.

Weight (mass) is measured in grams (g) and kilograms (kg). 1 kilogram = 1000 grams.

Capacity, the amount of liquid a container holds, is measured in litres (L) and millilitres (mL). 1 litre = 1000 millilitres. A bucket of water is measured in litres, while a spoon of medicine is measured in millilitres.

Choose the unit that fits the size of the thing being measured: the distance between two villages in kilometres, the length of a pencil in centimetres.
